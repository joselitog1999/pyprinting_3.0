## Summary of Key Historic Decisions

### DEC-001: Decoupling Power Flipper from Shutter Safety Lines
* **Context**: Previously, moving the neutral density (ND) optical flipper relied on shared digital lines, causing race conditions when emergency shutters triggered.
* **Decision**: Actuate the flipper strictly via analog voltage steps (+5V) on `Dev1/ao0` and `Dev1/ao1`, completely isolated from digital lines `port0/line0:3`.
* **Outcome**: Shutter panic close is unconditionally preserved regardless of flipper motion state.

### DEC-006: Real `QThread` for LineScanSpectroscopyWorker
* **Context**: Every existing PySpectrum routine (`growth_kinetics.py`, `hyperspectral_confocal.py`, `dimers.py`, `luminescence.py`) runs its `Backend(QObject)` on the GUI thread, driven by a `QTimer` whose steps are short (~20ms) and non-blocking. The new linescan routine's steps can legitimately block from hundreds of ms (single-window mode) to several seconds/minutes per spatial point (Step & Glue mode: multiple grating moves, each gated by `wait_until_ready()`, plus exposure time). A software-architect + instrumentation review of the blueprint found that running this in the GUI thread would risk the shutter heartbeat and the emergency-abort button going unresponsive during a blocking `time.sleep()`/polling call.
* **Decision**: `LineScanSpectroscopyWorker(QObject)` is moved to a dedicated `QThread` via `moveToThread()` (see `create_linescan_routine()` in `pyspectrum/modules/routines/linescan_spectroscopy.py`). The worker never relies on `hardware_session.emergencyStopSignal` to interrupt an in-progress blocking wait (a Qt signal is only delivered when the receiving thread returns to its event loop); instead it polls the thread-safe `hardware_session.is_emergency_stopped` property between every blocking sub-step, mirroring the `_abort_requested` pattern already used in `step_and_glue.py`. `heartbeat_shutter(30.0)` is called on every tick of any blocking wait (piezo settle polling, grating settle polling, exposure wait), not just once per step, since a single Step & Glue point can exceed the watchdog's 30s timeout.
* **Outcome**: First routine in PySpectrum 3.0 to use a real `QThread`. GUI remains responsive and the local/global abort paths remain effective during arbitrarily long acquisitions. Verified via `tests/test_linescan_h5.py` (9/9 passing) and the full `pytest tests/` suite (176 passed, no regressions).

### DEC-007: Formal ADR — Dedicated `QThread` Worker for the Linear Scan Spectroscopy Routine

* **Decision ID**: `DEC-007`
* **Title**: Dedicated `QThread` (`moveToThread`) Worker for the Linear Scan Spectroscopy Routine — First Departure from PySpectrum's QTimer-on-GUI-Thread Convention
* **Date**: 2026-09-16
* **Status**: `ACCEPTED`
* **Deciders**: Software Architect, Instrumentation

**1. Context & Problem Statement**
PySpectrum 3.0 has five acquisition routines. Four of them — `growth_kinetics.py`, `hyperspectral_confocal.py`, `dimers.py`, `luminescence.py` — run a `Backend(QObject)` on the GUI thread, driven by a `QTimer` whose steps are short (~20 ms) and non-blocking. The new Linear Scan Spectroscopy routine (`linescan_spectroscopy.py`) breaks that assumption: a single spatial point can block for hundreds of ms (Single-Window mode) to several seconds or minutes (Step & Glue mode), stacking piezo settle waits, one or more grating moves gated by `spectrometer.is_moving()`, and camera exposure time. Running this on the GUI thread would starve the Qt event loop during each blocking call: neither the shutter watchdog heartbeat nor the emergency-abort button could be serviced, since `pyqtSignal` delivery — including `hardware_session.emergencyStopSignal` — only happens once the receiving thread returns to its event loop.

**2. Decision Drivers**
* Shutter watchdog heartbeat and emergency-abort must stay serviceable through acquisitions that can exceed the watchdog's 30 s heartbeat timeout by orders of magnitude.
* GUI responsiveness (`SYS-101`) must hold under sustained blocking hardware polling.
* Reuse already-validated abort and HDF5 patterns over new abstractions.
* Preserve Liskov substitution on the HDF5 container schema.

**3. Considered Options**
* **Option A — Keep the `QTimer`-on-GUI-thread convention**: consistent with the other four routines; rejected because a single blocking sub-step (grating settle, exposure) can exceed the heartbeat cadence by orders of magnitude, leaving shutters and abort unresponsive.
* **Option B — `AcquisitionStrategy` class hierarchy** for the two acquisition modes: rejected as over-engineering; `step_and_glue.py` already resolves the same two-mode dilemma with sibling methods and no base class.
* **Option C (chosen) — `LineScanSpectroscopyWorker(QObject)` on a dedicated `QThread`** via `moveToThread()`, polling `hardware_session.is_emergency_stopped` between every blocking sub-step instead of trusting signal delivery mid-wait, mirroring the `_abort_requested` pattern already used in `step_and_glue.py`.

**4. Decision Outcome**
Chosen option: **Option C**, the only one that keeps the watchdog and abort paths responsive through arbitrarily long blocking waits without adding unneeded abstractions. `create_linescan_routine()` builds `LineScanSpectroscopyWidget` + `LineScanSpectroscopyWorker`, calls `worker.moveToThread(thread)`, wires widget→worker request signals and worker→widget result signals, then starts the thread. `_should_abort()` (`_cancel_requested or hardware_session.is_emergency_stopped`) gates `_sleep_with_heartbeat()`, `_move_and_settle()` (piezo, `pi.qONT()` polling, 5 s timeout) and `_settle_wavelength()` (grating, `spectrometer.is_moving()`, 6 s timeout); each re-issues `heartbeat_shutter(30.0)` per tick, not just once per step. Exposure wait adds `ACQUISITION_READOUT_MARGIN_S` (0.05 s, a documented placeholder — no real EMCCD readout model exists yet). Persistence uses the standalone `write_linescan_spectroscopy_hdf5()`, not a `BatchHDF5Container` subclass, reusing only its `shuffle+gzip(4)` compression under an independent hierarchical schema.

*Positive Consequences*: first PySpectrum routine with a verified-responsive abort/watchdog path across arbitrarily long acquisitions; no blind `time.sleep()` — every settle wait is hardware-polled with an explicit timeout and post-settle verification (`pi.qPOS` vs. `PIEZO_POSITION_TOLERANCE_UM`); zero LSP violation on the HDF5 layer.

*Negative Consequences / Trade-offs*: introduces PySpectrum's first real `moveToThread()` lifecycle, requiring explicit teardown (`_cleanup()` in `create_linescan_routine()`, plus `PySpectrumWindow.closeEvent()`) that the `QTimer` routines never needed; `ACQUISITION_READOUT_MARGIN_S = 0.05 s` remains an undocumented-physics placeholder pending a real EMCCD readout model; the two acquisition modes diverge structurally (sibling methods, no shared interface) — the same shape already used in `step_and_glue.py`.

**5. Physical / Code Traceability**
* **Affected Modules**: `pyspectrum/modules/routines/linescan_spectroscopy.py` (`LineScanSpectroscopyWorker`, `LineScanSpectroscopyWidget`, `create_linescan_routine`), `core/hdf5_container.py` (`write_linescan_spectroscopy_hdf5`), `tests/test_linescan_h5.py`.
* **Linked Compendiums**: `SYS-101`, `SYS-102`, `SYS-104`; complements `DEC-006`.

### DEC-008: Metrological Traceability of Transmittance/Extinction Datasets in LineScan Spectroscopy
* **Context**: Peer review of `linescan_spectroscopy.py`'s T/E pipeline and its HDF5 serialization (`core/hdf5_container.py::write_linescan_spectroscopy_hdf5`), specifically whether reusing `sif_processor.py`'s shared functions and storing an unclamped raw dataset alongside a derived `_physical` one is metrologically defensible.
* **Decision / Review Verdict**: Reusing `compute_transmittance_with_errors()`/`compute_extinction()` (shared with `sif_analyzer.py`) is the correct traceability choice — both modules derive T/E from the same audited implementation rather than parallel reimplementations. The raw-vs-physical split is metrologically sound: clamping raw T would bias a symmetric dark-noise residual (a GUM violation), so an unclamped raw dataset plus a derived `transmission_*_physical` copy is consistent practice, and the NaN gate (weak reference) is properly decoupled from the positivity clamp (signal sign) — two orthogonal guards, not conflated.
  Two non-blocking gaps were found during the initial review: (1) `transmission_*_physical = clip(T, 0, None)` floored at exactly `0.0`, not the `1e-6` epsilon `compute_extinction()` uses internally — a non-Python consumer computing `-log10()` directly on `_physical`, as its own `rationale` attribute invited, would hit `+inf` rather than a finite value. Note that `extinction_1d/2d` in this pipeline are still computed from the raw `T`, not from `_physical` — the derived dataset is written for external/downstream consumers but is not itself consumed anywhere in this pipeline; this remains true after the fix below and is not itself a defect (the raw dataset is metrologically the correct source for extinction). (2) The noise multiplier and per-row `noise_threshold_2d` were not persisted to HDF5, only inferable from `noise_threshold_1d`/`sigma_dark_1d`.
* **Outcome**: Core design **ACCEPTED — FULLY RESOLVED (2026-09-17)**. Both gaps closed:
  1. `EPSILON_TRANS = 1e-6` (`linescan_spectroscopy.py`) now floors `transmission_1d_physical`/`transmission_2d_physical` in `run_scan()`, matching `compute_extinction()`'s internal epsilon exactly. `write_linescan_spectroscopy_hdf5()` stamps `clipping_floor = 1e-6` alongside `clipping_applied = True` and an updated `rationale` attribute stating the epsilon explicitly and clarifying that rigorous metrological calculations must consume the raw `transmission_*` dataset, not `_physical`.
  2. `LineScanSpectroscopyWorker.acquire_reference()` now stores `noise_threshold_2d` (per-row, `mult * sigma_dark_2d`) in `self._reference` and tracks `self.noise_multiplier`; `write_linescan_spectroscopy_hdf5()` persists it as `/reference/noise_threshold_2d` (shape `[N_roi_y]`); `run_scan()` writes `noise_multiplier` (float, e.g. `3.0`) into `/metadata` attrs.
  Verified against `tests/test_linescan_h5.py` (10/10 passing — `test_transmission_physical_never_negative` updated to assert the `1e-6` floor and `clipping_floor` attribute; new `test_noise_threshold_2d_and_multiplier_persisted` asserts presence/shape of `/reference/noise_threshold_2d` and the `/metadata` `noise_multiplier` attribute). Full suite re-run before and after the fix confirms 0 regressions attributable to this change (`tests/` full collection: 177 passed both times when isolated from a pre-existing, unrelated set of 7 failures/2 errors caused by a known environment-level `PyQt6` DLL loading issue in `test_hardware_dashboard_logic.py`, `test_pyspectrum_stability_safety.py`, and `test_shutter_alignment_and_heartbeat.py` — reproduced identically with and without this change via `git stash`, confirming no causal link).

### DEC-009: Hardware-Safety Audit Remediation (P0) — Slit Clamp, Grating Settle Polling, Session Arbitration, Partial-Scan Reporting
* **Context**: A full 5-phase hardware-readiness audit of `pyspectrum/` (panels: `instrumentation`, `software-architect`, `qa-ux-auditor`, `metrology`) evaluating readiness to connect to the real Andor Shamrock SR-500i / iXon3 EMCCD / PI E-517 bench, prior to `DEC-009`. The audit returned a 🔴 `PELIGRO_DE_HARDWARE_BLOQUEADO` verdict on four independent P0 findings, detailed below, plus a root-caused test-hygiene bug that was masking the audit's own verification runs.
* **Decision — 4 patches applied, diagnosis-only findings NOT applied (logged as P1/P2 follow-ups)**:
  1. **P0-A (`pyspectrum/drivers/shamrock_driver.py::ShamrockDriver.set_slit()`)**: the real driver passed slit width straight to `self._dll.ShamrockSetSlit()` with **no clamping** — only `_MockShamrock` clamped to `10.0–2500.0 µm`, a mock/real parity violation and a hardware-safety-gate violation (CLAUDE.md §4: physical interlocks must never be bypassable). Fixed by clamping identically in the real driver before the DLL call, so an operator entering an out-of-range slit width in `spectrum_control.py`/`calibration_dock.py` can no longer drive the motorized blades against their mechanical hard-stop.
  2. **P0-B (`pyspectrum/modules/step_and_glue.py`, both `ShamrockSetWavelength()` call sites)**: used a hardcoded `time.sleep(0.05)` instead of confirming grating settle — 6× under the driver's own `WAVELENGTH_SETTLING_TIME_S = 0.3 s` constant. Every point of a Step & Glue panoramic scan was captured while the wavelength micrometer could still be moving, a systematic and silent spectral-smearing artifact. Fixed by adding `Backend._settle_wavelength()`, a direct port of `linescan_spectroscopy.py::LineScanSpectroscopyWorker._settle_wavelength()` (`is_moving()` polling, `heartbeat_shutter(30.0)` renewed per tick, bounded by a 6 s timeout, abort-aware via `self._abort_requested`), replacing both sleep sites.
  3. **P0-C (`pyspectrum/modules/step_and_glue.py::Backend.measure_single_spectrum()`)**: never called `hardware_session.acquire_session()` — only `measure_step_and_glue()` did. A "Single Spectrum" click could run concurrently with any other session-holding routine, racing on the shared Shamrock/Andor driver handles that `hardware_session` exists to arbitrate. Fixed by wrapping the method body in `acquire_session("Step & Glue — Espectro Único")` / `release_session(...)` (`try/finally`), matching the pattern already used in `measure_step_and_glue()`.
  4. **P0-D (`pyspectrum/modules/routines/linescan_spectroscopy.py::run_scan()`)**: if `_move_and_settle()`/`_settle_wavelength()` hit their own hardware timeout mid-scan (as opposed to a user cancel or E-STOP), the step loop `break`s but neither `_cancel_requested` nor `is_emergency_stopped` becomes `True` — so execution fell through the post-loop guard, wrote the (now partially-`NaN`) HDF5, and emitted `scanCompletedSignal` → a genuine "Escaneo Finalizado" success dialog for a corrupt dataset. Fixed with a new `self._scan_failed` flag, set **only** in the genuine-timeout branch of `_move_and_settle()`/`_settle_wavelength()` (never in their `_should_abort()` branch, which is already correctly handled by the existing cancel/E-STOP guard). `run_scan()` now stamps `metadata['scan_aborted']` and, when the flag is set, emits a new `scanAbortedSignal(h5_path, reason)` instead of `scanCompletedSignal` — the widget's new `on_scan_aborted()` slot shows a distinct "Escaneo Incompleto — Fallo Mecánico" warning, never the success dialog. The partial HDF5 is still written (not discarded) for forensic recovery of the valid points preceding the failure.

### DEC-011: PI E-517 Stage Connection Resilience — GCSError Isolation, MOV Clamping, Grid Pre-Flight Range Check, Pause/Resume on Real Disconnection
* **Context**: The PI E-517 piezo stage was disconnecting into irreversible "Modo Virtual" mid-unattended-experiment (overnight grid printing, long scans, drift tracking). Root cause: `config.py::_PIController.MOV()`/`qPOS()`/`qONT()` wrapped every call to `pipython.GCSDevice` in a bare `except Exception: self._connected = False`, so (a) a firmware-level command rejection (`GCSError -1004 "Position out of limits"`, triggered whenever a grid computation — `startX`/`startY` + drift-corrected `shiftx`/`shifty` — landed a fraction of a micron outside `[0, 100] µm`) and (b) a transient bus-busy read collision during an active `qONT()` polling loop were both indistinguishable, at the driver level, from the USB cable being physically unplugged. `is_physically_connected()` additionally sent `*IDN?` over the USB bus on every position-refresh poll, adding bus contention during active motion. None of this happened in the legacy driver, which never tore down the connection on a command-level error.
* **Decision**:
  1. **`config.py::_PIController.MOV()` (and `_MockPI.MOV()`, for testability — `pipython` isn't installed in this dev/test environment, so `pi` is always `_MockPI` here)**: unconditional clamp to `[0.0, PI_STAGE_RANGE_UM]` before the coordinate ever reaches the device, logging `[PI Driver Clamped]` when a value was actually out of range. This is the single point that eradicates `GCSError -1004` at the source, regardless of whether any individual caller (`modules/confocal.py`, `modules/measurements.py`, etc.) remembered to clamp itself.
  2. **`GCSError` isolation**: `MOV()`/`qPOS()`/`qONT()` now catch `GCSError` (imported from `pipython`, with a local fallback `class GCSError(Exception)` when `pipython` isn't installed, so the `except GCSError:` clause is always syntactically valid) as its own branch that logs and **never** touches `self._connected`. Only a **non**-`GCSError` exception — a genuine low-level communication failure — can still lead to a disconnection.
  3. **Retries, `qPOS()`/`qONT()`**: new `_retry_read()` helper retries a failed read up to 2 times (20 ms apart) before giving up. On persistent failure, `qPOS()` returns the last known cached position (`self._pos`) and `qONT()` assumes on-target — **neither ever sets `self._connected = False`**, per explicit design: a read hiccup during active motion (the documented collision scenario) is not evidence of a dead connection.
  4. **`is_physically_connected()`**: no longer sends `*IDN?` on every call. Uses `GCSDevice.IsConnected()` (a host-side state query with no USB traffic) when available, falling back to the in-memory `self._connected` flag otherwise.
  5. **`try_auto_reconnect()`**: new method, called from `MOV()`'s non-`GCSError` exception branch — one transparent `connect()` attempt before `self._connected` is finally set to `False`. Only a `MOV()` failure that is both (a) not a `GCSError` and (b) survives a failed auto-reconnect attempt now results in a real disconnection — the narrowest possible trigger, matching "solo error irrecuperable de comunicación" from the mission brief.
  6. **Grid pre-flight range check, `modules/measurements.py::Backend`**: new `_preflight_grid_range_check()`, called at the top of `_grid_start()` (before any `pi.MOV()` or `open_shutter()`), computes the grid's total bounding box (`startX`/`startY` ± `grid_x`/`grid_y` extent, plus a 3 µm thermal-drift margin) and aborts — emitting the new `gridRangeErrorSignal(str)` → `Frontend._show_grid_range_error()` (a `QMessageBox.warning`) — if it exceeds `[0, PI_STAGE_RANGE_UM]`. This catches a scientifically-distorted grid (edge particles silently clamped to a different position than calculated) *before* any laser opens, which the driver-level clamp in #1 alone cannot do (it protects the hardware, not the experiment's geometric validity). Also added an explicit redundant clamp at `target_insitu_x`/`target_insitu_y` (Stage 3/4, in-situ autofocus) per the mission brief, defense-in-depth alongside #1.
  7. **Pause/resume state machine on real disconnection**: new `Backend._check_physical_connection_or_pause()`, called at the top of `_grid_move()` (the single choke point every particle transition passes through). If `pi.connected` is `False` (now only possible after the narrow #2–#5 trigger), it calls `close_all_shutters()` immediately, sets `is_paused = True` and `mode_printing = "none"` (deliberately *not* touching `i_global`, `node_results`, or any drift-history list, so progress survives the pause automatically since nothing resets those attributes), and emits the new `stageDisconnectedSignal(str)` → `Frontend._show_stage_disconnected_dialog()`, a modal with a dedicated "🔌 Reconectar y Reanudar" button wired to the new `Backend.resume_after_reconnect()` slot, which calls `pi.connect()` and — on success — re-enters `_grid_move()` at the still-pending `i_global` node. On failure, re-emits the same signal with an updated message so the operator can retry.

### DEC-012: Universal Template Matching and Crystallographic Generalization for Hexagonal and Honeycomb Lattices in 2D Disorder Analyzer
* **Context**: `core/lattice_disorder.py`/`analysis/lattice_disorder_gui.py` (Fase 1) only supported square/rectangular Bravais lattices, analyzed via axis-aligned integer-index KDTree matching (`analyze_real_space_kdtree`). The lab needed to characterize two additional high-priority nanolithographic geometries: hexagonal/triangular monoatomic lattices ($\gamma=60°$, $Z=6$) and honeycomb/graphene lattices (2-atom basis, subrefs A/B, bond coordination $Z=3$), neither of which fits an axis-aligned integer-index scheme. The mission asked for a universal engine that (a) consumes `core/lattice_generator.py`'s already-validated `LatticeLayer`/`BasisAtom`/`BoundingGeometry`/`CrystalGridComposer` classes as the ideal-template source instead of reimplementing lattice generation, and (b) performs rigid registration (translation + rotation) + KDTree matching with sublattice decoupling for the analysis side, which `lattice_generator.py` does not provide (it only generates ideal templates, never registers them against noisy experimental data).
* **Decision**:
  1. **`generate_ideal_lattice_template(lattice_type, a, b, gamma_deg, boundary_type, boundary_size_nm, rotation_deg, ...)`**: thin bridge to `core.lattice_generator.CrystalGridComposer` — builds a `LatticeLayer` (µm-converted `a`/`b`, `gamma_deg`=60° auto for hex families, atoms from `BasisAtom`), delegates cell expansion/rotation/boundary-clipping to the existing composer, converts nodes back to nm, and labels each node with `sublattice_id` from `material_id`.
  2. **Honeycomb basis bug found and worked around, not fixed at the source**: numerically verified that `LatticeLayer._default_basis_for_type()`'s honeycomb/graphene basis ($u=1/3, v=2/3$), combined with the $\gamma=60°$ convention `grid_generator.py` applies to hexagonal families, does **not** produce a valid honeycomb (4 distinct bond distances in the first shell — $0.2a, 0.4a, 0.529a \times 2$ — instead of 3 equidistant neighbors at $a/\sqrt{3}$). The correct fractional basis for this $\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2$ convention is $u=1/3, v=1/3$ (verified: exactly 3 neighbors at $a/\sqrt{3}$, then 3 at $a$). `generate_ideal_lattice_template()` uses the corrected basis locally via an explicit `atoms=[...]` override; `core/lattice_generator.py` itself is **not modified** (out of scope — it also drives real sample fabrication via `grid_generator.py`, so any honeycomb sample already printed with the stock basis should be re-checked against $a/\sqrt{3}$; recommended as a follow-up task).
  3. **`register_and_match_template(x_real, y_real, template_x, template_y, template_sublattice, ...)`**: rigid registration via centroid alignment + a 2-stage (coarse exhaustive sweep → fine refinement) rotation search minimizing mean-squared nearest-neighbor residual — deliberately **not** a local-gradient optimizer, per the mission's own falsification concern that a naive optimizer can lock onto a neighbor-spacing-periodic local minimum instead of the true mounting misalignment ($1$–$5°$ tolerance is realistic). Followed by bounded KDTree matching (`max_dist_nm`, default half the template's median nearest-neighbor distance) with per-particle sublattice labeling and **independent vacancy accounting per subred** (`n_vacancies_by_sublattice`).
  4. **`compute_voronoi_topology(..., ideal_z=4)`** generalized with an `ideal_z` parameter (default 4, backward-compatible) so `defects_mask = coordination != ideal_z` works for `ideal_z=6` (hexagonal) and `ideal_z=3` (honeycomb — verified numerically that a honeycomb lattice's *geometric* Voronoi coordination, dominated by the 3 much-closer bond neighbors, really is 3, not 6; this coincides with but is computed independently from the bond coordination).
  5. **Real bug found and fixed in the Fase-1 vertex-merge heuristic (`_merge_close_polygon_vertices_xy`)**: the original sequential seed-at-`vertices[0]` merge algorithm silently failed whenever a degenerate vertex cluster of 3+ points straddled the polygon array's wrap-around boundary (index `k-1 → 0`) — common in honeycomb cells (3-vertex triangles, higher per-vertex sensitivity to positional noise than 4+-constrained square cells) but rare enough in square lattices to have gone undetected in Fase 1's own regression test. Measured: only ~50-58% of honeycomb cells correctly resolved to $Z=3$ under 3% bond-length noise. Replaced with a topologically robust cycle-graph formulation (count "far" — i.e. non-degenerate — edges around the vertex cycle; immune to where the array happens to start/end) plus raising the merge tolerance from 10% to 20% of the median nearest-neighbor distance (triangular honeycomb cells have less geometric redundancy than square cells' 4+ constraints, so the same absolute positional noise displaces a Voronoi vertex proportionally more) — verified this doesn't hide the pre-existing real-vacancy-defect regression test. Result: honeycomb $Z=3$ detection rose to 96%+ under the same noise level.
  6. **`compute_bond_orientational_order(..., n_fold=None)`** generalized to optionally compute a generic $\psi_n$ (e.g. `n_fold=3` for honeycomb's native 3-fold bond symmetry) alongside the existing $\psi_4$/$\psi_6$, returned under `psi_n_local`/`psi_n_mean`.
  7. **`compute_basis_structure_factor(Gx, Gy, sublattice_offsets_nm)`**: analytic $|F(\mathbf{G})|^2 = |\sum_\kappa e^{-i\mathbf{G}\cdot\mathbf{d}_\kappa}|^2$ for an $N$-atom basis (honeycomb: $F=1+e^{-i\mathbf{G}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}}$), provided as an independent geometric prediction; the effect itself already emerges naturally from the existing NUFFT ($S(f_x,f_y)$ computed directly over both sublattices' real positions), no multiplicative term needed there.
  8. **`extract_angular_profile(S, fx, fy, angle_deg, band_width_bins)`** and **`compute_radial_azimuthal_profile(S, fx, fy, n_r_bins)`** (the latter fully vectorized via `np.bincount`, no per-angle Python loop): generalize the existing fixed-45°-diagonal cartesian cut extraction to arbitrary angle and to full azimuthal integration $S(q)$, needed for hexagonal 0°/60°/120°-family symmetry cuts.
  9. **Real bug found and fixed — hexagonal reciprocal-lattice orientation**: `run_hexagonal_monte_carlo_calibration`'s first draft assumed the 6 equivalent first-order Bragg directions sit at $0°,60°,120°,\ldots$ (aligned with the real-space lattice vectors). Numerically brute-forced the true Bragg peak location against `generate_ideal_lattice_template`'s actual output and found it at $-30°$ — the reciprocal lattice of a triangular Bravais lattice is rotated 30° relative to the real one (standard crystallographic result, but not assumed correctly on the first attempt). The wrong angles produced a **monotonically increasing** (physically backwards) Debye-Waller attenuation curve with $\sigma$; corrected to $-30°,30°,90°,150°,210°,270°$, now monotonically decreasing with $R^2>0.998$. Same fix applied to the Pestaña 3 (reciprocal space) hexagonal Bragg-marker overlay and radial-profile ring in the GUI.
  10. **`run_hexagonal_monte_carlo_calibration(lattice_type, a, boundary_type, boundary_size_nm, ...)`**: new, separate from (not bolted onto) `run_monte_carlo_calibration` — reuses `generate_ideal_lattice_template` for the bounded ideal grid, injects vacancies/noise with the same statistical scheme as Fase 1, evaluates $S$ at $f_0=2/(\sqrt{3}a)$ averaged over the 6 corrected Bragg directions via a single vectorized `np.outer` + complex-sum BLAS-friendly evaluation (no Python loop over direction/frequency), fits via the existing (unmodified) `fit_debye_waller_curve`.
  11. **Real crash fixed in a pre-existing Fase-1 function, exposed (not caused) by hexagonal data**: `compute_analytical_bragg_relations` raised `ZeroDivisionError` (`1.0/aniso_ratio` unguarded, only the denominator of `aniso_ratio` itself was guarded) whenever `sigma_wilson_x` degenerated to exactly 0 — which happens routinely when cartesian Wilson-plot analysis (designed for square/rectangular peaks on the fx/fy axes) is run against hexagonal data whose true Bragg peaks aren't on those axes. Fixed with a second guard (`max(1e-6, aniso_ratio)`); `_on_recalculate_reciprocal()` in the GUI now also fully replaces the cartesian-specific metrics text (anisotropy/Hx/Hy/Wilson jargon, meaningless for this geometry) with hexagon-appropriate text (detected ring radius vs. $f_0$, correct Bragg directions) instead of leaving the misleading cartesian numbers on screen.
  12. **GUI (`analysis/lattice_disorder_gui.py`)**: Pestaña 2 gained a "Tipo de Red y Geometría Envolvente" group (lattice-type/boundary-type/boundary-size/auto-rotate/manual-rotation controls, shown only for hex families) that dispatches `_on_recalc_grid()` between the Fase 1 rectangular KDTree path and the new template-matching path via an adapter (`_convert_template_match_to_kdtree_dict`) that maps `register_and_match_template`'s output onto the exact dict schema the rest of the pipeline (topology plots, histograms, metrics table) already consumes — no downstream rendering code needed forking. Subred A/B rendered in distinct colors on the topology canvas. ### DEC-018: PySpectrum 3.0 Rework Fase 4 — Raised-Cosine Step & Glue, Grating-Aware Step Calculation, 2D Support, Water Raman Reference Check and HDF5 Export
* **Context**: The Fase 4 brief asked to recover a "legacy `StepandGlue_ps.py`" cosine-blending algorithm — no such file exists anywhere in this repository (confirmed by a full-tree search before writing any code), so the brief's own explicit mathematical formula (the raised-cosine $w_1=\cos^2(\theta)$, $w_2=\sin^2(\theta)$ pair) was treated as the authoritative specification rather than a file to locate. The brief also asked for a configurable 10-50% overlap SpinBox, grating-aware step-center calculation, 1D/2D multimodal support, halogen normalization, a water Raman O-H reference check, and HDF5 export.
* **Decision**:
  1. **A second, distinct glue algorithm was added alongside the existing `glue_steps()`, not a replacement**: reading `pyspectrum/calibration/halogen_lamp.py::glue_steps()` before writing anything revealed it already does a sigmoidal-style blend — but a **logistic sigmoid** ($w=1/(1+e^{-x})$ over a fixed ±3.5 window) with a hardcoded 30-pixel edge trim, mathematically different from the brief's raised-cosine formula and with `w1+w2=1` only holding approximately (asymptotically) rather than exactly at every point. `glue_steps()` is used unmodified elsewhere (`linescan_spectroscopy.py`, its own existing test `test_sigmoidal_step_and_glue_monotonicity`) so replacing it in place would have been a silent behavior change to code outside this phase's scope. Verified `w1+w2=1` holds to floating-point precision (`~2.2e-16`) at every point, not just at the endpoints.
  2. **Overlap boundaries are derived from the real intersection of consecutive steps' wavelength axes, not from the requested overlap percentage**: `glue_pair_sigmoidal(wave1, spec1, wave2, spec2)` computes $\lambda_a=\max(\min(wave_1),\min(wave_2))$, $\lambda_b=\min(\max(wave_1),\max(wave_2))$ directly from the actual calibrated axes returned by the spectrograph for each step — self-consistent regardless of any small mismatch between the *planned* step spacing and the *actual* λc the hardware settled at, avoiding a second, potentially inconsistent, "expected overlap" bookkeeping path. The `overlap_pct` SpinBox instead parametrizes `compute_step_centers()` (how far apart to space the λc requests), which the blend function then adapts to automatically.
  3. **Grating dispersion bug found and fixed, not just extended**: the pre-existing step-center calculation hardcoded a `240.0` nm "grating span" regardless of which grating (150 or 1200 l/mm) was actually active — inconsistent with `shamrock_driver.py`'s own real dispersion constants (0.175 nm/px for 150 l/mm ⇒ ~176 nm full-CCD span, 0.022 nm/px for 1200 l/mm ⇒ ~22 nm), confirmed by re-reading the Shamrock driver rather than trusting the existing comment. `compute_step_centers(start_wl, end_wl, overlap_pct, grating, num_pixels)` now derives the span from `GRATING_DISPERSION_NM_PER_PX` (same constants as `_MockShamrock.ShamrockGetCalibration`), so the 1200 l/mm grating correctly demands far more steps (~29 vs. ~4 for 150 l/mm covering the same 450-950nm range at 20% overlap, verified numerically) instead of using an unrelated fixed span.

  5. **Full 2D glued matrix is cached and exported separately from its 1D display projection**: the first draft of the 2D path only kept the row-mean (needed for the existing 1D `PlotWidget`) and would have silently exported only that 1D projection to HDF5 under `/glued_spectrum`, contradicting the brief's explicit "matriz cosida de ultra-alta resolución $[H\times W_{total}]$" requirement — caught and fixed by adding a separate `self._last_frame_2d` cache that `export_hdf5()` prefers over the 1D mean when present.

### DEC-032: Verified Live and Single Raman Acquisition
* **Context**: `DEC-031` left `toggle_live(True)` without a `finally`, framed as "if `start_acquisition()` raises after the shutter opened, the shutter stays open". Being a change to shutter control flow, it fell in `CLAUDE.md` §5.0's never-exempt class and went through Rounds 1-2 — which is where the framing turned out to be **wrong**, and where the protocol earned its place on what looked like a three-line change.
* **Round 1 finding that invalidated the obvious fix**: `AndorCCDDriver.start_acquisition()` reports failure through its **return code** (`DRV_NOT_INITIALIZED`, or whatever `StartAcquisition()` returns), not through an exception. The proposed `try/finally` would therefore have caught almost nothing: the realistic failure was a code that `toggle_live` **discarded outright**, after which it opened the spectrograph shutter and started the live timer anyway, with the camera not acquiring. The real defect was the same one `DEC-014` forbids — software state running ahead of unconfirmed hardware — one line below the shutter call that `DEC-031` had just fixed.
* **Two further Round 1 findings**:
  * **No return path for the live state existed.** The widget emitted `toggleLiveRamanSignal` and the backend consumed it; nothing flowed back. A failed start left the button reading "Detener Live" with nothing running, and the Inspector's button and `Ctrl+Space` out of sync — the `DEC-013` class of defect generalised.
  * **A defect introduced by `DEC-031` itself.** The window routes `statusMessageSignal` to `statusBar().showMessage(msg, 4000)`, which shows only the latest message. `DEC-031`'s helper emitted its shutter warning and the caller then emitted "Espectro único adquirido exitosamente." immediately after — so the warning existed in code but was overwritten before an operator could read it. Recorded here plainly: the previous entry's fix did not work as described.
* **Operator decisions (Round 1 questions)**: (A) if the camera starts but the shutter does not confirm, keep live running with a warning rather than aborting; (B) `StartAcquisition()` does **not** begin integrating until the first frame is fetched, which makes a camera-first start order physically safe; (C) extend verification to single acquisition as well.
* **Implementation**:
  * `toggle_live(True)` starts the camera **first** and checks the code against `DRV_SUCCESS` (a driver exception is treated as the same failure). Only on success does it open the shutter and start the timer. A failed start therefore never leaves the shutter open — structurally, not by cleanup, because it was never opened. `DRV_ACQUIRING` gets an explicit hint that Exploration's Live View is probably still running on the shared detector.
  * New `StaticRamanBackend.liveStateChangedSignal(bool)` carries the state **actually reached**, wired in `make_connection()` to the spectrum widget and the Inspector. `StaticRamanWidget` gained a public `set_live_state()` that updates the button under `blockSignals` so it cannot bounce back into the backend; the old `_on_toggle_live()` both repainted and emitted, so calling it from the backend would have looped.
  * `_set_spectrograph_shutter()` gained `report=False`, and both `acquire_single()` and `toggle_live()` now compose a **single authoritative final message** emitted last, instead of every step overwriting the previous one.
  * **Decision (C) rested on a wrong premise, stated here rather than glossed over**: `acquire_single()` never calls `start_acquisition()` at all, so there was no code to check. The failure signal that *does* exist there is that the real driver returns an all-zero array from `get_1d_spectrum()`/`get_most_recent_image()` on any read error. A physical EMCCD never yields an identically zero frame (bias offset alone is hundreds of counts), so `_acquire_and_emit()` now returns whether the data is non-zero and `acquire_single()` reports an all-zero frame as invalid rather than as success.

These paths are **unreachable against the mock** — it always returns success — so the tests inject spy camera and spectrograph objects that fail on demand and record call order; they wrap instances rather than patching the mock singleton, so they cannot leak into other files. **Negative control**: re-binding the previous `toggle_live` and `acquire_single` onto the class makes **11 of the 13 fail**. The two that still pass are the ones that should: the widget-setter test exercises code that was not reverted, and the valid-acquisition test is the happy path the old code also handled.

* **Deliberately out of scope, still open**: nothing in `pyspectrum/` sets the Andor acquisition mode (single scan vs run-till-abort), and `acquire_single()` reads the most recent image without starting an acquisition. On real hardware that may return a stale frame from a previous live session. Fixing it changes the acquisition protocol itself, which is never exempt — recorded for its own deliberation. The two other items left open by `DEC-031` (the unverified detector pitch; `ShamrockSetShutter` returning success from its own `except`) are unaffected. **[Both since resolved: `DEC-033` and `DEC-034`.]**

### DEC-033: Detector Pitch Is 8 µm — and the Wrong Value Was Planning Step & Glue, Not Just Drawing an Overlay
* **Context**: `DEC-031` consolidated the detector geometry into one symbol but left open *which* pitch was right: 13 µm live in `DETECTOR_PIXEL_PITCH_UM` (backed by `SYS-301`/`SYS-303`/`SYS-305`, none citing a source) against 8 µm in a dead Shamrock constant. It was framed as an overlay-scale question. Rounds 1-2 of `CLAUDE.md` §5.0 (never exempt: a physical scale) showed it was much larger than that.
* **Evidence (six independent sources, all pointing to 8 µm)**: the Andor iXon3 885 datasheet (1004 × 1002 px, 8 × 8 µm, "VP" front-illuminated sensor); the TI TC285SPD-30 datasheet (1004 × 1002, 8.0 µm); the two Solis `.sif` files in `reserva/`, whose headers — written by Solis from the camera itself — give head model `DU8285_VP` and 1004 × 1002 (the `_VP` suffix is the datasheet's sensor option, so `DU8285` is the real identifier, not a typo); the legacy `Instrument_Shamrock_ps.py:22-24` (`#Camera Andor 885`, `PixelWidth = 8`, fed to the Shamrock SDK to compute λ since 2020); the legacy Step & Glue window of 103 nm (`StepandGlue_ps.py:576`), which equals the Shamrock 500i datasheet's nominal dispersion for the installed 150 l/mm blaze-800 grating (12.83 nm/mm) times 1004 × 8 µm = 8.032 mm; and Solis's own Step & Glue in those `.sif` files, 5020 = 5 × 1004 points over 400–900 nm. `SYS-303` additionally called the sensor back-illuminated and listed dispersions (0.301 / 0.035 nm/px) consistent with no pitch at all.
* **What the wrong value was actually doing** — five places, one of them serious:
  1. The slit overlay drew the band 38 % too narrow (the known effect).
  2. **Step & Glue planning.** `halogen_lamp.GRATING_DISPERSION_NM_PER_PX = {1: 0.175, 2: 0.022}` planned 176 / 22 nm windows against real 103 / 11.6 nm. Measured with the real planner: at the default 20 % overlap, **29.8 % (150 l/mm) and 39.3 % (1200 l/mm) of the requested range would never have been measured** on hardware. The legacy "optical core" (103 / 12 nm), which 3.0 presented as a conservative estimate, was the true full window.
  3. The spectral-range badge estimated ~176 nm before the first acquisition.
  4. **The mock used the same 0.175 / 0.022**, which is why no test ever saw (2): planner and simulator shared one wrong number, so simulation was self-consistent.
  5. **PySpectrum 3.0 never told the Shamrock SDK the detector geometry.** The legacy calls `ShamrockSetNumberPixels` + `ShamrockSetPixelWidth` at start-up (`Spectrum_ps.py:157-158`); 3.0 called `ShamrockGetCalibration` in ~15 places and neither setter, leaving every wavelength axis on real hardware dependent on state nobody set. 3.0 had not yet been used in production (operator), so no 3.0 data needs correcting.
* **Operator decisions**: accept 8 µm on this evidence without waiting for a bench `GetPixelSize`; full scope (constant + single-source dispersion + SDK configuration + planning from the real calibration); precedence operator's "Zona Óptica Central" > measured window > datasheet nominal; fix the planner's stop rule; correct the misleading tooltip as documentation (no GUI contract change, so no Round 3).
* **Implementation**:
  * `DETECTOR_PIXEL_PITCH_UM = 8.0`. `shamrock_driver` gains `NOMINAL_DISPERSION_150_NM_PER_MM = 12.83` and `NOMINAL_DISPERSION_1200_NM_PER_MM = 1.44` (datasheet, gate-verified in `lab-invariants.md`), `nominal_dispersion_nm_per_px()` and `nominal_window_nm()`; the dead `PIXEL_WIDTH_UM` is gone and `NUMBER_OF_PIXELS` is now an alias of `DETECTOR_WIDTH_PX`.
  * Real driver and mock gain `set/get_number_pixels`, `set/get_pixel_width` and `configure_detector_geometry()`, sharing one `_configure_and_verify_geometry()` so both walk the same protocol: set, **read back**, and only then `geometry_verified = True` (`DEC-014`). `get_shamrock()` configures every instance before anyone can ask for a calibration; every initialisation path, including `core/hardware_manager.py`'s reconnect, goes through it. On failure the real driver is **kept** with `geometry_verified = False` and a loud log line — hiding a live spectrograph behind the mock would be worse. The legacy passed 1002 (the vertical axis); 3.0 passes 1004.
  * The mock derives its calibration and cubic coefficients from its own stored pixel width, so a wrong pitch now shows up in simulation instead of being masked.
  * `halogen_lamp.resolve_step_window_nm()` implements the precedence; `compute_step_centers(..., window_nm=)` plans from it; `StepAndGlue.Backend._measured_window_nm()` reads the span from the calibration at the current position and returns `None` (→ nominal) when geometry is unverified, the code is an error or the axis is degenerate. The new `coverage_gaps_nm()` verifies coverage afterwards against the **real axes** of every window; gaps go to the log and to the HDF5 (`window_nm`, `window_source`, `coverage_gaps_nm`).
  * **Planner stop rule, refined during Round 4 verification.** The approved rule — add windows only until coverage reaches the end — was implemented, and the end-to-end test then found a residual 0.4 nm unmeasured at the *start*: real windows are not symmetric about their centre (dispersion curvature, grating positioning accuracy), and the glue crops 15 edge pixels. Internal overlaps absorb that; range edges had no margin. Each edge now gets the same margin as either side of an internal overlap (half the overlap). Recorded as a refinement of the approved rule, found by its own test.
* **Tests that encoded the wrong physics, corrected**: `test_dispersion_shamrock_500i` asserted a 160–190 / 18–26 nm window (13 µm) and so certified the mock's error; the three `test_optical_core_*` tests asserted the core window yields *more* steps, true only against 176 nm; `test_custom_30_percent_overlap_yields_more_steps` asserted a strict step-count increase that is a discretisation artefact (now asserts denser spacing). Each now checks the real property.
* **Verification**: `tests/test_spectral_geometry_and_step_coverage.py`, 30 tests. Its oracle is the **datasheets as literals**, not the code's constants — a test that derives its expectation from the constant it checks would let a wrong value confirm itself, which is exactly how 13 µm survived. **Negative control** against the unmodified code: every test failed except `test_mirror_grating_terminates` (an invariance guard that must pass on both); the behavioural failures included the mock window (175.70 nm vs 103.05 nm), its cubic dispersion (0.175 vs 0.10264 nm/px) and the pitch constant, the rest failing on the missing API. `test_planning_with_the_old_13um_window_is_caught` reproduces the historical defect on the corrected mock and proves the coverage check reports it. **Mutation checks** for the guards that needed the new API: each of the five mechanisms — factory configuration, read-back verification, the `geometry_verified` gate on the measured window, the coverage check, the edge margin — was removed in memory, and its test failed every time while passing on the intact code.

### DEC-005: Event Isolation via `_results_stale` Flag in Lattice Disorder Analyzer
* **Context**: An internal audit (computational-physicist, metrology, qa-ux-auditor) found that `_update_real_space_analysis()` in `analysis/lattice_disorder_gui.py` silently cascaded Bloque 5 (cluster detection), Bloque 6 (grid/vacancy fit) and Fourier reciprocal-space recalculation from 12+ unrelated call sites (particle deletion, undo/revert curation, ROI apply, image invert, and — most critically — `_on_load_image()`, which could recompute clusters/grid/g(r) against a newly loaded image while still holding `locs_df` positions from the previous image).
